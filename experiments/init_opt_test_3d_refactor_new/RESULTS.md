# Insights and conclusions

**All 1512 cells completed; 0 failures.** `mode=opt` means every number here is the
*exact* posterior's, so nothing below is a statement about a model — it is a statement
about the importance-weighted estimator at each curvature.

The grid was collected in two passes: `K` ∈ {-10, -1, -0.01} first (504 cells), then the
six curvatures `setup.md` added — -4, -3, -2, -0.5, -0.1, -0.05 (1008 cells). The second
pass is what turns a three-point bracket into a curve, and it **changes the conclusion**:
see §3.

## 1. The reference pass is exactly invariant, as designed

`wnelbo_ref`, `nelbo_ref`, `wce_ref` and `ce_ref` are byte-identical across all 14
(loss_geometry × rate) cells of every `(ps, K, seed)` group — now **108 groups**, max
relative spread **2.2e-16 (one ulp)**; `wce_ref` and `ce_ref` are bit-identical. Only
`wloss`, the swept objective, varies. The `salt=0` / `salt=1` RNG split in
`BaseTrainer._make_step_generator` does what it claims, and it keeps doing it at six

## 3. The variance cliff is necessary but nowhere near sufficient — the pre-registered threshold is wrong

`EXPERIMENT.md` predicted, from the `1/R^2` scaling of the ~0.304 cliff, that the
breakdown would appear where the pinned `exp(0.1)` reference first crosses the cliff:
`R^2 = 3.04`, `K ≈ -0.33`, i.e. **between `K = -0.5` and `K = -0.1`**. The six added
curvatures were chosen to bracket exactly that. **They do not find it there.**

| ref 0.1, as a multiple of the cliff | 0.03–0.66× (`K` −10…−0.5) | 3.3× (`K` = −0.1) | 6.6× (`K` = −0.05) | 33× (`K` = −0.01) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| observed deficit | none | none (±1%) | −0.8 … −2.2% | −25 … −28% |

At `K = -0.1` the reference proposal is already **3.3× past the infinite-variance cliff**
and the estimator is still correct to within seed noise. The cliff is where the *variance*
stops existing; it is not where a 4M-sample mean stops being usable. Crossing it costs
precision first — `wnelbo_ref_std` is U-shaped in `K`, minimised at 3.3–4.9 at `K = -0.5`
and rising in both directions — and only turns into visible downward bias once the
proposal misses enough of the integral to matter, somewhere between 3.3× and 6.6× the
cliff.

The practical boundary at `d = 3` with the pinned `exp(0.1)` reference is therefore
**`K = -0.1` (`R^2 = 10`)** — the usable range runs `-10 <= K <= -0.1`, two decades of
curvature, where the cliff argument would have stopped at `K = -0.33`. The mechanism in §4
is unchanged and still explains the sign and the size of the collapse; what is refuted is
using the cliff crossing as the *usability* threshold. It is pessimistic by ~6.6× in `R^2`
for the onset (20 vs 3.04) and ~33× for the collapse — it condemns curvatures that are in
fact fine.

**Caveat on the `!` flags in the tables above.** `experiments/report.py` marks loss
proposals against a *fixed* 0.304 cliff, which is the `K = -1` value. The cliff is
`0.304 · R^{-2}`, so those flags are correct only in the `K = -1` sections: they
under-flag every `K` flatter than −1 and over-flag every `K` sharper. That is shared
machinery used by all four `*_refactor_new` projects, so it is flagged here rather than
changed unilaterally. It affects only the `wloss` column's annotation — no reported number
depends on it.

## 4. The mechanism: the integral moves, the proposal does not

`K` fixes `R = 1/sqrt(-K)`, and the path-KL integrand is a *rate* carrying `kappa^2 = -K`.
Flattening the curvature does two opposite things at once:

- the integrand shrinks — visible in `nelbo_ref`, which falls monotonically from 0.0491 at
  `K = -10` to 0.0035 at `K = -0.01` for `naive_ps`;
- the range it must be integrated over grows as `R^2`, since `main_refactor.py`'s
  heat-time ceiling is `_radial_t_max(3) · R^2` = 9.7 → 97 → 9696.

The product is conserved — that is why `H(p)` is still the right answer — but the pinned
`exp(0.1)` reference draws `t` with mean 10 and weights it by `e^{0.1 t}/0.1`. The bulk of
a 9696-long integral is never sampled, and the estimator returns roughly the fraction it
did see. The nine-curvature sweep shows that fraction degrading *smoothly*: full at
`R^2 <= 10`, 98–99% at `R^2 = 20`, 72–75% at `R^2 = 100`.

`ce_ref` corroborates the same picture from the other side, and now monotonically across
all nine curvatures: for `naive_ps`, 0.0062 → 0.0558 → 0.1002 → 0.2783 → 0.3589 → 0.4654
nats as `K` goes −10 → −1 → −0.5 → −0.1 → −0.05 → −0.01. At the `t` the proposal actually
draws, the flatter the bridge, the less it has concentrated and the more ambiguous the word
still is. Note that `ce_ref` rises steadily through the whole range, including the region
where `wnelbo_ref` is still exact — so a large `ce_ref` is a warning sign, not a
disqualification.

## 5. `c1e3` and `c1e4` agree to all printed digits

At every one of the nine curvatures, the two geometric-law specs return identical
`wnelbo_ref`, `wce_ref`, `nelbo_ref` and `ce_ref`. This reproduces the known
configuration-independence of the Bayes-optimal Girsanov integrand documented in
`init_refactor_3d/EXPERIMENT.md`. As there: **they are one validation, not two independent
ones** — do not quote them as mutual corroboration. Six new curvatures did not perturb it.

## 6. What this licenses the trained projects to claim

- For `K` from **−10 down to −0.1**, `test_wnelbo_ref >= H(p)` is a valid test of the
  trained model, and the trained project's distance from `H(p)` is the model's own. The
  trained twin `init_test_3d_refactor_new` sweeps `K` ∈ {−10, −1, −0.01}, so two of its
  three curvatures are cleared, and cleared with far more margin than the three-point grid
  could show.
- At `K = -0.05`, subtract the ~1–2% estimator deficit before reading a trained cell; a
  trained model within 2% of `H(p)` there is at the estimator's floor, not the model's.
- At `K = -0.01`, `H(p)` is the wrong reference entirely. A trained cell must be compared
  to **0.3594 / 1.2439 / 0.7667**; reading it against `H(p)` would report a leak that is
  not there.
- Measuring the ELBO at a flat curvature needs the reference proposal re-tuned to that
  curvature. The re-tuning target is now measured rather than assumed: the pinned rate is
  intact at 3.3× the cliff and degraded at 6.6×, so the empirical requirement is
  `rate <~ 1.0 · R^{-2}` — about 3× more permissive than the `0.304 · R^{-2}` cliff. That is a change to `setup.md`'s design,
  so it is flagged here rather than made unilaterally.
- Six of the nine curvatures have no trained counterpart. They measure the estimator only,
  and that is what they were run for.
