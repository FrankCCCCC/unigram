## What d = 16 shows

- **The reference estimator is the limiting factor at d = 16.** Its
  importance-weighted std is 30–100 (vs 4 at d = 3), and it is heavy-tailed
  enough that the *std estimate itself* doubled between 1M and 32M samples. A
  4M-sample test pass has SE ≈ 0.02–0.05 per cell, and all cells with one
  (seed, max_steps) share their test sample, so step-groups swing by ±0.05 (the
  non-monotone rows above). Differences below ~0.05 here are not resolvable.
- **On naive_ps the Bayes model reads H (0.5032 ± 0.0053); on cmplx_ps it reads
  +0.05 above H (1.7186 ± 0.0171).** With the tail behaviour above that is
  nominally 3 SE but not a safe call; a small bias of the tabulated even-d
  sampler at small t (where the whole d = 16 ELBO lives — the drift is 7.5, so the
  word is identified by t ≈ 0.3) is the candidate to check next, e.g. by a
  32M-sample Bayes run with the exact `sample_radial` (≈ 12 h at 3.3 s per call)
  or by tightening the table (`_RADIAL_TABLE_PER_DECADE`, `_RADIAL_TABLE_NU`).
- **CE training and the polar ELBO decouple at high d.** `wce_ref` is 0.01–0.03:
  the CE objective has almost nothing left to fit once the word is identified,
  which happens almost immediately, while the polar ELBO is dominated by
  t ≲ 0.3 where the model must output the *prior-like* posterior. CE-trained
  naive_ps cells get *worse* with training (0.51–0.52 at 20k → 0.53–0.54 at 200k
  for every rate ≥ 0.05), PP-trained cells reach 0.50–0.51. At d ≤ 3 the
  informative-t window is wide and the two objectives agree.
- **The exp(0.01) loss proposal is the wrong tool at high d** (0.60 / 0.68 for
  CE / PP on naive at 200k): its draws sit at t ~ 100, far beyond
  `_radial_t_max(16) = 1.5`, so training samples land on the clamp where the
  loss is 0. Rates 0.5–1.0 — above the d = 2 variance cliff — are the *best* at
  d = 16, because the loss decays so fast in t that the weighted estimator's
  variance is finite there. The variance cliff is dimension-dependent; the
  `!` markers in the tables (calibrated at d = 2) do not apply here.
- `nelbo_ref` (unweighted) is 0.05 / 0.17 on naive / cmplx at every d ≥ 3:
  the per-t loss under the proposal is dimension-independent at this level; it
  is the weights that change the picture.

## What to change before a d = 16 follow-up

1. Reference proposal matched to d (rate ~ 1 at d = 16) and/or `test_size`
   ≫ 4M, plus an independent eval seed per run — see
   `init_refactor_3d/RESULTS.md` for the shared-draw mechanism.
2. Pin the d = 16 Bayes reference with the exact sampler before reading
   ±0.05 effects.
