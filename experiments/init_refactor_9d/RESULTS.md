# Insights and conclusions

*Sweep complete (336 / 336 cells, 2026-08-25). Regenerate the tables with
`python experiments/report.py init_refactor_9d`; this section is preserved.*

- **The bound holds at d = 9.** Bayes-optimal model (`mode=opt`, 32M samples):
  see the d = 9 rows of the cross-dimension summary in
  `experiments/init_refactor_16d/RESULTS.md`. No trained cell is below H beyond
  the estimator's noise.
- **The estimator is ~5× noisier than at d = 3.** `test_wnelbo_ref_std` ≈ 23 at
  the Bayes model (4.3 at d = 3), so a 4M-sample test pass has SE ≈ 0.012 per
  cell, and every cell with the same (seed, max_steps) shares its test sample
  (see `init_refactor_3d/RESULTS.md`), so whole step-groups move together by
  ±0.02. Treat differences below ~0.02 in these tables as noise.
- **What the training says, rate-averaged (rates ≤ 0.25, 12 cells):** CE-trained
  cells reach H within noise by 200k steps on both datasets (naive 0.503 ± 0.011,
  cmplx 1.676 ± 0.011); PP-trained cells the same (0.514 ± 0.026, 1.672 ± 0.010)
  with the exception of the exp0.01 loss proposal, which is clearly worse for PP
  (0.556 at 200k): its mean draw t = 100 sits far beyond `_radial_t_max(9) = 5.6`,
  so almost every training sample lands on the clamp where the loss is 0.
- **`wce_ref` collapses**: 0.03 (naive) / 0.09 (cmplx) for CE-trained cells vs
  2.2 / 6.7 at d = 2 — the `(d-1)/2` drift identifies the word almost
  immediately, so the denoising cross-entropy integral is tiny.
