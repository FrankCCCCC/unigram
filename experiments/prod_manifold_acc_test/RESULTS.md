- Each cell is **`mean / variance`**, both averaged across the 3 seeds, as
  setup.md's "Result Presentation" asks: `mean` is the point estimate

  4,000,000-draw test pass in nats² (avg of `test_<metric>_std**2`).

  variance in any existing run; filling them needs STD_KEYS extended and a re-run.
- These variances are NOT the `± std` these tables used to print. That was the
  across-seed spread of the mean, related by `± ≈ sqrt(variance / 4,000,000)`.
- `!` marks loss proposals above the ~0.304 variance cliff, where the
  weighted estimator has infinite variance. The reference pass is pinned at
  exp(0.1) and stays valid, but training there is materially noisier.
- `wce_ref` is dominated by rare extremes; treat its spread as indicative only.

# Insights and conclusions

756 / 756 cells completed, zero failures. Per-cell wall clock: 35-62 s for the
V = 10 datasets and 325-462 s for `c1e4_exp1.0`, against the pre-acceleration
cost model's ~250 s / ~720 s.

## 1. The tensorized product-manifold path reproduces the loop implementation

**CONFIRMED.** The 126 `-0.01x-10.0x-1.0` cells re-run
`init_opt_test_3x3d_refactor_new`'s published grid, which was computed with the
per-factor python loops. The samplers now consume the RNG in a different order,
so the two are expected to agree within Monte-Carlo error of the 4,000,000-sample
test pass, not bit for bit. Over all 126 matched cells, with
`z = (new - old) / sqrt((std_new^2 + std_old^2) / 4e6)`:

| metric | max \|z\| | cells beyond 3 sigma | mean \|z\| | max relative change in per-sample std |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wloss` | 1.90 | 0 / 126 | 0.59 | 2.99% |
| `wnelbo_ref` | 0.88 | 0 / 126 | 0.28 | 1.27% |
| `wce_ref` | 0.48 | 0 / 126 | 0.31 | 1.05% |
| `nelbo_ref` | — | — | — | 1.62% (relative difference of the mean) |
| `ce_ref` | — | — | — | 0.78% (relative difference of the mean) |

Not one cell deviates by more than 1.9 sigma. The per-sample variances -- which
are what a change in the sampler's law would move first, and which no unit test
covers -- agree to within 3%. Together with the unit checks (deterministic parts
to <= 2e-15 against the loops), the acceleration is accepted.

## 2. Curvature moves the ESTIMATOR, not the bound

**CONFIRMED.** `wnelbo_ref` is within 0.5-4% of `H(p)` at every curvature, and
every dip below `H(p)` is inside the estimator's own noise -- at uniform -0.01
the per-sample variance is 545 / 4239 / 6940 nats^2, i.e. a standard error of
0.012 / 0.033 / 0.042, and the observed shortfalls are 0.028 / 0.043 / 0.023.
The bound is intact; the sampling is what changes.

What curvature *does* move is the variance, and it does so sharply. Per-sample
variance of `wnelbo_ref`, averaged over seeds (reference proposal pinned at
exp(0.1)):

| geometry | naive_ps | cmplx_ps | c1e4_exp1.0 | `ce_ref` (naive_ps) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| uniform -0.01 | 544.7 | 4238.8 | 6939.7 | 0.4131 |
| **uniform -0.05** | **7.2** | **16.8** | **10.9** | 0.2337 |
| uniform -1.0 | 42.9 | 107.9 | 62.6 | 0.0203 |
| uniform -3.0 | 124.8 | 319.2 | 184.3 | 0.0069 |
| uniform -10.0 | 409.6 | 1045.3 | 606.1 | 0.0021 |
| mixed [-0.01,-10,-1] | 177.1 | 437.1 | 265.2 | 0.0060 |

The variance is **U-shaped with a clear minimum at K = -0.05**, 76x below the
K = -0.01 cell and 57x below K = -10 on `naive_ps` (252x and 62x on `cmplx_ps`).
This is the slides' "curvature controls which noise levels receive training
mass" made quantitative: for `d = 3` the dimensionless radius is
`u ~ t |K|`, so at the pinned exp(0.1) proposal (mean `t = 10`) the typical
`u` runs 0.5 at K = -0.05 and 100 at K = -10, while the ELBO integrand is
bounded by `2 (d-1)^2 kappa^2 = 8 |K|`. Flat curvature wastes the proposal on
uninformative small-`u` draws; sharp curvature inflates the per-sample scale.
K = -0.05 is where the two costs cross for this proposal.

**Practical consequence:** the reference estimator is not equally trustworthy
across the curvature sweep. Comparing `wnelbo_ref` at K = -0.01 against
K = -0.05 at fixed `test_size` compares a standard error of 0.012 against one of
0.0013. Either scale `test_size` with the variance, or scale the reference
proposal rate with curvature so `u` -- not `t` -- is what is held fixed.

## 3. The mixed geometry does NOT track its tightest factor

**REFUTED as stated, with a sharper replacement.** The prediction was that
`[-0.01, -10.0, -1.0]` would track uniform -10.0, because `main_refactor` clamps
the heat time to the MINIMUM over factors of `_radial_t_max(3) R_m^2` -- 9.7,
set by the K = -10 factor. The clamp does behave that way, but the OBSERVABLES
do not: the mixed cell lands between uniform -1.0 and uniform -10.0 on every
metric (`ce_ref` 0.0060 against 0.0203 and 0.0021; variance 177 against 43 and
410).

The reason is that the clamp and the information run in opposite directions.
Under the shared ceiling `t <= 9.7`, each factor sees its own dimensionless
radius `u_m ~ t |K_m|`: the K = -10 factor reaches `u ~ 97`, the K = -1 factor
`u ~ 9.7`, and the K = -0.01 factor only `u ~ 0.097` -- which is no information
at all. Information ADDS across factors (their Busemann terms sum), so the
product behaves like roughly ONE sharp factor plus one moderate one, not like
three sharp ones: its `ce_ref` of 0.0060 sits well above the 0.0021 that three
K = -10 factors deliver. The tightest factor sets the time BUDGET; it does not
set the answer.

**Practical consequence:** a heterogeneous product pays for its flattest factor
twice -- that factor contributes almost nothing, and its presence does not
relax the ceiling its sharpest sibling imposes. Mixed-curvature products are
worth it only when the curvature spread is small enough that every factor still
reaches an informative `u` inside the shared clamp.

## Caveat carried forward

`nelbo_ref` and `ce_ref` still have no logged per-sample variance
(`trainer.BaseTrainer.STD_KEYS` covers the three weighted quantities only), so
the control comparison for those two is a relative difference of means rather
than a sigma. Extending `STD_KEYS` would close that gap on the next re-run.
